The ingredient story

An old answer to a modern problem.

For generations, tallow was the moisturizer — rendered slowly from suet and kept in a jar by the washbasin. It fell out of fashion not because it stopped working, but because cheaper, water-based lotions were easier to mass-produce. We render ours in small batches from grass-fed suet, whip it with olive oil for a smooth, spreadable texture, and finish it with a whisper of frankincense.

  • Grass-Fed Beef Tallow

    Naturally rich in fats similar to those found in skin's own barrier, which is why it absorbs so readily and moisturizes so deeply.

  • Extra Virgin Olive Oil

    Softens the whip to a silky, balm-like texture and adds its own centuries-old reputation for conditioning skin.

  • Organic Frankincense Essential Oil

    A quiet, resinous, faintly woody note — grounding rather than perfumed, and gone within minutes of applying.

How to use

A little, warmed, wherever skin is thirsty.

Start small

Scoop a pea-sized amount with a clean, dry finger. Tallow is concentrated — half of what you'd use of a lotion is plenty.

Warm it

Melt it between your fingertips for a few seconds. It softens instantly into a smooth, glide-on balm.

Press it in

Massage into damp skin after a shower or washing your face. Give it a few minutes to absorb fully — then forget about dry skin for the day.

Common questions

Everything people ask before their first jar.

Does it smell like beef?

No. Properly rendered tallow is nearly odorless. Ours is slowly wet-rendered and filtered, then finished with a trace of frankincense — the overall effect is faintly woody and resinous, and it fades within minutes of applying.

Will it clog my pores?

Tallow's fat profile is remarkably close to the oils your skin produces on its own, and most people — including many with breakout-prone skin — find it absorbs cleanly. Every skin is different, though: start with a small amount at night and see how your skin responds over a week.

Is it suitable for sensitive skin?

It was made with sensitive skin in mind. There are no synthetic fragrances, preservatives, dyes, or seed oils — just three recognizable ingredients. If you're sensitive to essential oils specifically, patch-test on your inner arm first, since it does contain frankincense.

How should I store it?

Keep the lid closed and store it at room temperature away from direct sun. Tallow is naturally shelf-stable — no refrigeration needed. In a hot house it may soften; in a cold one it firms up. Either way it's fine, and it melts on contact with skin regardless.

How long does a jar last?

Longer than you'd expect. Because it's concentrated fat rather than mostly water, a pea-sized scoop covers your whole face. Used daily on the face, a 2 oz jar typically lasts two to three months. Best used within a year of opening.

Can I use it on my face, or just my body?

Both. Most of our customers use it as a nightly face moisturizer, and it doubles for hands, elbows, heels, lips, cuticles, and anywhere else that runs dry. One jar, whole routine.
